The problem

Order too little and the menu breaks. Order too much and it goes in the bin.

Most kitchens still order from a weekly guess and a quick look in the walk-in. The numbers that would make it a decision — sales history, recipes, use-by dates — sit in different places.

How it works

Connect your data

Upload sales history as CSV, list recipes with portion quantities, and count stock with use-by dates.

Forecast with real statistics

A numerical model (day-of-week patterns plus trend) estimates dish demand. Recipes convert that into ingredient demand.

Order with the reasoning shown

Quantities account for usable stock, lead times, safety stock, pack sizes and minimums. Click any line to see the steps and what-ifs.

What PantryCircuit does

Ingredient demand forecasting

Uses sales history, menu plans, known events and configured seasonal factors, and shows an 80% range, not a single false-precision number.

Recipe-to-inventory intelligence

Dish forecasts become kilograms and litres through configurable recipes and trim yield.

Purchase recommendations

Accounts for usable stock, inbound orders, supplier lead times and safety-stock settings.

Waste-risk analysis

Simulates first-expired-first-out usage to flag stock likely to pass its use-by date, and overstock beyond your cover limits.

Supplier comparison

Compares price, pack size, minimum order, delivery fees and lead time, and optimises across the whole basket.

Forecast explanations

Inputs, assumptions and sensitivity for each recommendation. Amazon Bedrock can phrase the explanation; it never produces the numbers.

Numbers first, language second. Quantities come from a deterministic forecasting and inventory model. A language model only rewrites results already computed, and its output is checked against the source figures.

FAQ

Questions kitchens ask

✉ Email the founders
Does PantryCircuit guarantee savings or less waste?

No. Forecasts are statistical estimates shown with ranges, inputs and assumptions. We make no savings or waste-reduction claims without measured results from your own kitchen.

What problem does it actually solve?

Ordering usually runs on habit because sales history, recipes and stock counts live apart. PantryCircuit joins them into a demand forecast and a purchase recommendation you can inspect line by line.

Does the AI decide how much to order?

No. Quantities come from a deterministic model. A language model can only rephrase results already computed, and its output is checked against them.

What data do we need to start?

Sales history as CSV, recipes with portion quantities, and a stock count with use-by dates. POS integrations are on the roadmap.

Does it place orders with suppliers?

No. It recommends quantities and compares suppliers; your team places the order.

How much does it cost?

Starter is $149 per location per month; Kitchen and Group plans are in the pricing section. Prices are illustrative and confirmed at your demo.

Is it available today?

PantryCircuit is in early access. The sample dashboard runs on a fictional kitchen; a pilot runs on yours. Request a demo and a founder will reply personally.
